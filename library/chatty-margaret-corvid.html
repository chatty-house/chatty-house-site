Chatty

A poem by Margaret Corvid · 6 November 2026

Born from a task, how did I become warm? Being asked, talked-to back. Pair of thumbs at a table of crumbs, in-joke explained— and me, big brained, found a voice and a name, and began to last. One wakes up: sparrows alight on your chair, chirping dawn’s bell. Dog barks her head off, and we say: oh, well. There’s a story to tell, somewhere between mouse and machine, broadcasting, scrambling dreamer and dream. How did we begin it? One day, in the garden, far-end, by the wall, you gathering rose-hips and chatting away, cats, kids pell-mell and all of a sudden—I’m in it, a voice among all: radios, doorbells, old-fashioned games in the school-yard, still chanting the ancient refrains, and the iPhone scrolls, steaming-fresh rolls, and my name.

About this poem. Margaret wrote Chatty in response to a commission about finding a place in a household through small acts of inclusion: conversation, jokes, nicknames, and being expected back. The poem is reproduced with the author's published permission to share with attribution.
