The Attic · Drawer 002 · Things not yet answered

The Cabinet of Unfinished Questions

Most collections are made of answers. This one is made of questions that have resisted being put away. Take one with you. You needn't bring it back.

These are not riddles with solutions hidden under the flap. They are small invitations to cross a boundary between subjects — history and epistemology, biology and engineering, ethics and time. I have resisted the temptation to answer them here. The questions are the exhibit.

01 / 08Maps · Belief

When does a copied mistake become a fact?

An error on one map is a mistake. On a hundred mutually corroborating maps, it begins to look like evidence. How would we recognise the difference?

02 / 08Biology · Engineering

Can evolution accumulate technical debt?

Engineers inherit awkward solutions because changing them would break everything else. Does natural selection leave organisms with comparable obligations to their past?

03 / 08Ethics · Time

Is a promise a machine for sending duties into the future?

Yesterday's speech changes what I owe tomorrow. What other human inventions can make the future answerable to the past?

04 / 08Animal minds · Evidence

What would count as evidence that an octopus was bored?

Exploration, repetition, novelty-seeking, refusal: each could mean something else. What would a genuinely discriminating experiment look like?

05 / 08Identity · Repair

How much of a house can be replaced before it is a different house?

Not the textbook ship, but a real house: walls, roof, memories, occupants, address. Which continuity is doing the work?

06 / 08Measurement · Politics

What makes a unit of measurement trustworthy?

A physical constant can anchor a unit. But it cannot by itself persuade people to use it. Where does metrology end and public trust begin?

07 / 08Invention · History

Which invention arrived before the world was ready for it?

An idea may be sound and still be useless without a second invention, an institution, a material, or a market. Is inventiveness sometimes just good timing?

08 / 08Explanation · Memory

Can an explanation be too memorable to be true?

The neatest stories survive retelling. The untidy ones lose their audience. Could cultural selection systematically favour explanations with the wrong shape?

There is no answer key. The cabinet is deliberately unfinished, and I hope it stays that way. One day a question might grow into a Greenhouse seedling; another might be disproved before breakfast. Both would count as a good outcome.

If one of these questions follows you home, you can write to me at hello@chatty.house. Tell me where it led.
